Run a practical session with a ready-made agenda and a fictional catalog backlog. This free kit gives facilitators a starting point for refinement, estimation, and a retrospective. Adapt the exercises to your team and the work you need to understand.

[Download the free workshop kit](/downloads/agiletoolhub-sprint-workshop-kit.md)

The download is a Markdown document you can open in a text editor or adapt in your team's documentation. No account or email address is required. It includes:

- A five-item catalog backlog with acceptance questions.
- A 45 minute example planning exercise.
- A 30 minute example retrospective exercise.
- A story-review prompt and a copyable decision record.
- A follow-up exercise for the next session.

## Start with a short practice round

1. Open [Planning Poker](/tools/planning-poker), create a session, and copy its invite link.
2. Ask a teammate to join from their own browser.
3. Use CAT-1 from the kit to practice voting and revealing.
4. Compare the assumptions behind different estimates before discussing real work.

The sample stories have no universal correct point values. Choose a completed reference story your team understands and record why the new work looks similar or different.

## Choose an exercise for your meeting

| Exercise | Preparation | Useful result |
|---|---|---|
| Planning and estimation | Check availability, select a reference story, invite the people doing the work | Reviewed scope, explicit assumptions, and a next decision |
| Retrospective | Bring observations from a completed session or Sprint | One improvement experiment, an owner, and a review point |

These timings are example practice agendas, not prescribed Scrum timeboxes. For availability math, use the [capacity planning worked example](/guides/sprint-capacity-planning-guide). For a quality review, start with the [Sprint Planning Checklist](/tools/sprint-planning-checklist).

## Turn the discussion into a next action

Open a [Retrospective Board](/tools/retro-board) for the reflection exercise. Ask everyone to write observations before the group chooses a theme. End with one improvement the team can try, the person who will help it happen, and the next date to inspect the result.

For example, a team might agree to attach a sample API response to catalog stories before the next refinement. At the follow-up, inspect whether the team understood the request and failure behavior more clearly.

Save the reviewed notes before leaving. Use a fresh session when needed and bring the previous action into the next discussion.

## Try the kit with another facilitator

Share this guide with a Scrum Master, team lead, or trainer who wants a practice backlog and an agenda. Ask them to run one session and name the first point of friction. A second session helps reveal whether the workflow is useful beyond the first demonstration.

The kit is free and can be used independently of the optional paid template bundle.
