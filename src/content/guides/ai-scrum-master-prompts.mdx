AI can help Scrum Masters prepare faster, summarize better, and spot gaps earlier. It should not replace facilitation.

The useful pattern is simple:

1. Bring a messy input: backlog items, standup notes, retro notes, stakeholder feedback, or sprint history.
2. Ask AI for a structured draft: checklist, table, risk list, prompt questions, or Jira-ready action items.
3. Review the result with the team before making decisions.

Scrum.org has also been writing about AI in Agile and Scrum, including practical uses for sprint planning and AI education for Scrum Masters. See [AI-Enhanced Sprint Planning](https://www.scrum.org/resources/blog/ai-enhanced-sprint-planning) and Scrum.org's [AI training announcement for Scrum Masters](https://www.scrum.org/resources/scrumorg-launches-new-ai-training-scrum-masters).

Use the prompts below as working drafts. Replace the bracketed parts with your actual context.

## Quick Rules for AI Scrum Prompts

Good AI Scrum Master prompts have five parts:

| Part | What to include | Example |
|------|-----------------|---------|
| Role | What the AI should act as | "Act as a Scrum Master helping a software team prepare sprint planning." |
| Event | The Scrum event or workflow | Sprint planning, daily scrum, backlog refinement, retro |
| Context | Safe, relevant input | Ticket titles, notes, risks, blockers, capacity |
| Output format | How the result should look | Table, checklist, agenda, Jira action items |
| Constraints | Rules the AI must follow | No new scope, flag assumptions, keep items testable |

Bad prompt:

```text
Help with sprint planning.
```

Better prompt:

```text
Act as a Scrum Master preparing sprint planning for a software team.
Review the backlog items below and return a table with:
1. Ready for sprint? yes/no
2. Missing acceptance criteria
3. Dependency or risk
4. Suggested question to ask the Product Owner
5. Suggested split if the item is too large

Backlog items:
[paste safe backlog summaries]
```

## Sprint Planning Prompts

Use these prompts before sprint planning, not during a rushed meeting.

### 1. Backlog Readiness Check

```text
Act as a Scrum Master preparing sprint planning.
Review these backlog items against Definition of Ready.

Definition of Ready:
- Clear user or business value
- Acceptance criteria included
- Dependencies identified
- Small enough for one sprint
- Testable outcome

Return a table with:
- Item
- Ready? yes/no
- Missing information
- Risk or dependency
- Question to ask before planning

Backlog items:
[paste ticket titles and short descriptions]
```

Use this before opening the [Sprint Planning Checklist](/tools/sprint-planning-checklist).

### 2. Sprint Goal Draft Prompt

```text
Act as a Scrum Master helping a Product Owner draft a sprint goal.

Inputs:
- Product objective: [objective]
- Candidate backlog items: [items]
- Team capacity: [capacity]
- Known risks: [risks]

Create 3 sprint goal options.
Each option should include:
- Goal statement
- Why it matters
- Which backlog items support it
- What not to include in this sprint
- Risk to discuss before commitment
```

Good output should help the team choose a focus, not just list tickets.

### 3. Capacity Risk Prompt

```text
Act as a Scrum Master reviewing sprint capacity.

Team context:
- Sprint length: [days]
- Team members and availability: [availability]
- Average velocity: [velocity]
- Planned ceremonies or holidays: [events]
- Support/on-call load: [load]

Identify:
1. Realistic sprint capacity
2. Overcommitment risk
3. People with limited availability
4. Work that should be deferred
5. Questions to ask before commitment

Return a concise table and a recommendation.
```

You can pair this with the [Sprint Capacity Calculator](/tools/sprint-capacity-calculator).

### 4. Planning Poker Discussion Prompt

```text
Act as a Scrum Master facilitating story point estimation.

For this backlog item:
[paste safe story summary]

Generate:
- Clarifying questions before estimation
- Hidden complexity to discuss
- Testing considerations
- Dependency risks
- Reasons one person might vote low
- Reasons one person might vote high

Do not assign a final estimate. Help the team have a better planning poker conversation.
```

Run the team vote in [Planning Poker](/tools/planning-poker), then use the AI output to guide discussion when estimates disagree.

## Daily Scrum and Standup Prompts

AI is useful after standup, especially for summarizing blockers and follow-ups.

### 5. Standup Summary Prompt

```text
Act as a Scrum Master summarizing daily scrum notes.

Turn the notes below into:
- Yesterday
- Today
- Blockers
- Dependencies
- Follow-up conversations needed after standup

Rules:
- Keep it brief
- Do not turn this into a manager status report
- Highlight blockers that need same-day action

Notes:
[paste safe standup notes]
```

You can also use the [Daily Standup Generator](/tools/daily-standup-generator) for quick Yesterday/Today/Blockers formatting.

### 6. Blocker Detection Prompt

```text
Act as a Scrum Master looking for hidden blockers.

Review these daily updates and identify:
- Explicit blockers
- Hidden blockers
- Dependency risks
- People who may need help
- Questions to ask after standup

Return a table with owner, blocker, urgency, and next action.

Updates:
[paste updates]
```

Hidden blockers often sound like:

- "Waiting for review"
- "Need clarification"
- "Still looking into it"
- "Should be done soon"
- "Blocked by environment"

## Backlog Refinement Prompts

Use AI to prepare refinement, not to bypass team discussion.

### 7. User Story Improvement Prompt

```text
Act as a Scrum Master and Product Owner reviewing a user story.

Story:
[paste story]

Improve it by returning:
- Clearer user story wording
- Acceptance criteria in checklist format
- Given/When/Then scenarios
- Assumptions
- Open questions
- Suggested story split if too large

Keep the original intent. Do not invent business requirements.
```

Try the [User Story Generator](/tools/user-story-generator) when you want a structured first draft from a rough feature idea.

### 8. Acceptance Criteria Gap Prompt

```text
Act as a QA-minded Scrum Master reviewing acceptance criteria.

Feature:
[feature summary]

Existing acceptance criteria:
[criteria]

Find:
- Missing happy path criteria
- Missing edge cases
- Error states
- Permission or role scenarios
- Data validation rules
- Testability gaps

Return improved acceptance criteria in Given/When/Then format.
```

You can generate a clean starting point with the [Acceptance Criteria Generator](/tools/acceptance-criteria-generator).

### 9. Epic Breakdown Prompt

```text
Act as a Scrum Master helping a team break down an epic.

Epic:
[epic summary]

Break it into sprint-sized user stories.
For each story include:
- Story title
- User story format
- Acceptance criteria
- Dependency
- Suggested order
- Reason this slice can be delivered independently

Avoid technical task lists unless needed to explain dependencies.
```

## Retrospective Prompts

AI can help cluster retro notes, but the team should still own the conversation.

### 10. Retro Theme Clustering Prompt

```text
Act as a neutral Scrum Master summarizing retrospective notes.

Group the notes into themes:
- What went well
- What needs improvement
- Action items
- Repeated signals
- One topic the team should discuss first

Rules:
- Do not blame individuals
- Keep original meaning
- Make action items specific and assignable

Retro notes:
[paste anonymized notes]
```

Use the [Retrospective Board](/tools/retro-board) to collect notes live, then use this prompt after the discussion to clean up action items.

### 11. Action Item Quality Prompt

```text
Act as a Scrum Master improving retrospective action items.

Current action items:
[paste action items]

Rewrite each action item so it has:
- Clear owner
- Specific next step
- Due date or review point
- Expected outcome
- How the team will know it worked

Flag vague items that are unlikely to be completed.
```

Weak retro action item:

```text
Improve communication.
```

Better:

```text
By next Wednesday, the team will add dependency notes to every ticket entering sprint planning. Scrum Master reviews this in refinement.
```

## Sprint Review Prompts

Sprint reviews are about inspecting the increment and adapting the backlog.

### 12. Sprint Review Prep Prompt

```text
Act as a Scrum Master preparing a sprint review.

Sprint context:
- Sprint goal: [goal]
- Completed items: [items]
- Not completed items: [items]
- Demo risks: [risks]
- Stakeholders attending: [stakeholders]

Create:
- 45-minute agenda
- Demo order
- Stakeholder questions
- Risks to mention honestly
- Backlog follow-up items after the review
```

### 13. Stakeholder Feedback Prompt

```text
Act as a Scrum Master and Product Owner reviewing sprint review feedback.

Feedback:
[paste stakeholder feedback]

Convert it into:
- New backlog items
- Clarification questions
- Product risks
- Items that should not become backlog work yet
- Suggested priority discussion for the Product Owner

Do not promise scope. Keep the output as planning input.
```

## Weekly AI Workflow for Scrum Masters

Here is a realistic weekly workflow that does not add much overhead.

| Day | Scrum Master use case | Prompt to use | Output |
|-----|-----------------------|---------------|--------|
| Before refinement | Check backlog readiness | Backlog Readiness Check | Missing info and PO questions |
| Before planning | Draft sprint goal options | Sprint Goal Draft | Goal options and risks |
| During estimation prep | Prepare discussion | Planning Poker Discussion | Clarifying questions |
| Daily | Summarize blockers | Blocker Detection | Same-day follow-ups |
| Before retro | Cluster notes | Retro Theme Clustering | Themes and candidate actions |
| After retro | Improve actions | Action Item Quality | Clear owners and outcomes |

## Privacy and Data Safety

Do not paste sensitive data into AI tools unless your organization explicitly allows it.

Avoid pasting:

- Customer names or private customer data
- Credentials, tokens, passwords, private keys
- Production incident secrets
- Security vulnerabilities that should stay internal
- Contract, finance, HR, or regulated data
- Full Jira exports with private comments

Safer input:

```text
Story A: User can reset password by email. Missing AC for expired token.
Story B: Admin can export CSV. Dependency on reporting API.
Story C: Checkout bug. Payment retry behavior unclear.
```

Unsafe input:

```text
Full customer ticket with names, email addresses, internal URLs, API tokens, logs, and private comments.
```

## Common Mistakes When Using AI in Scrum

### Mistake 1: Letting AI decide the estimate

AI can suggest discussion points, but the team estimates. Story points are a team calibration tool.

Use [Planning Poker](/tools/planning-poker) for the vote and AI for better questions.

### Mistake 2: Accepting generated acceptance criteria without review

AI often misses product context. The Product Owner, developers, and QA should review any generated criteria.

### Mistake 3: Turning standup into a status report

AI summaries are useful, but the daily scrum is still for team coordination. Do not use AI summaries to create surveillance reports.

### Mistake 4: Creating too many action items

Retrospectives fail when action items are vague or too numerous. Pick one or two specific improvements.

### Mistake 5: Pasting sensitive data

Clean the input first. If the prompt still works after removing private details, use the cleaned version.

## Copy-Paste Prompt: Full Scrum Master Assistant

Use this as a general-purpose prompt when you are not sure where to start.

```text
Act as a pragmatic Scrum Master for a software team.

Context:
[paste safe context]

Scrum event or workflow:
[sprint planning / daily scrum / backlog refinement / sprint review / retrospective]

Help me prepare by returning:
1. Main risks or gaps
2. Questions to ask the team
3. Questions to ask the Product Owner
4. Suggested next actions
5. Any Jira-ready follow-up items

Rules:
- Do not invent scope
- Flag assumptions clearly
- Keep the output concise
- Make action items specific and assignable
- Protect team collaboration; do not turn this into a status report
```
