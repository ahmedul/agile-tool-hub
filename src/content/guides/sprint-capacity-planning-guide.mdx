Sprint capacity planning makes upcoming availability visible before the team chooses work. Start with the calendar, subtract known commitments in consistent units, and discuss what the remaining time means for the Sprint Goal. Available hours help a forecast; they do not guarantee completed features.

Use the [Sprint Capacity Calculator](/tools/sprint-capacity-calculator) to make availability assumptions visible, or use the hours-based table below. For a group exercise, the [free sprint workshop kit](/guides/free-sprint-workshop-kit) includes an agenda and sample backlog.

## The capacity formula

```text
Scheduled team hours = sum of each person's scheduled hours in the Sprint
Available hours = scheduled hours - holidays - leave - meetings - other commitments
Planning allowance = available hours - an explicit reserve, if the team chooses one
```

Use the same unit for every term. Meeting hours across five people must be compared with scheduled hours across the same five people. If a holiday is already excluded from scheduled working days, do not subtract it again.

## Worked example for five people

This fictional team has five people scheduled for ten weekdays at eight hours per day. A public holiday falls on one of those weekdays and affects everyone. Alex also takes two other days of leave; Blair takes one. Each person has four hours of planned meetings. Casey and Devon each have eight hours of support duty.

| Person | Scheduled hours | Holiday | Leave | Meetings | Support | Available hours |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alex | 80 | 8 | 16 | 4 | 0 | 52 |
| Blair | 80 | 8 | 8 | 4 | 0 | 60 |
| Casey | 80 | 8 | 0 | 4 | 8 | 60 |
| Devon | 80 | 8 | 0 | 4 | 8 | 60 |
| Ellis | 80 | 8 | 0 | 4 | 0 | 68 |
| **Total** | **400** | **40** | **24** | **20** | **16** | **300** |

```text
5 people × 10 weekdays × 8 hours = 400 scheduled hours
400 - 40 holiday - 24 leave - 20 meetings - 16 support = 300 available hours
Example reserve of 10% = 30 hours
Example planning allowance = 270 hours
```

The ten percent reserve is a choice for this example, not a Scrum rule. A team with frequent unplanned support may choose another approach. If support is already represented by a reserve, do not also subtract the same support hours as a separate commitment.

## Check the roles and dependencies behind the total

Three hundred available hours does not mean every combination of work is feasible. A story may depend on a specialist who is away, a review that cannot happen until Friday, or another team's release.

Review the likely work against the people and skills needed to finish it. If all selected stories require Casey's review, Casey's availability can be a constraint even when other team members have unused time. Pairing and shared ownership can change that constraint; they do not create extra calendar hours.

## Capacity and velocity answer different questions

| Planning input | What it describes | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Upcoming capacity | Availability and known commitments in the next Sprint | Check whether the proposed work fits the people and calendar |
| Historical velocity | Points completed in comparable past Sprints | Understand the team's own delivery history and variation |
| Sprint Goal | The outcome the team wants to achieve | Choose a coherent set of work and discuss useful tradeoffs |

If a team completed 28, 32, 30, and 30 points in comparable Sprints, its arithmetic average is 30 points. That history is a starting discussion, not a promise to complete 30 points next time. Leave, changing team composition, unfamiliar work, and dependencies can change the forecast.

The [Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html#sprint-planning) describes past performance, upcoming capacity, and the Definition of Done as inputs that help Developers forecast. It does not prescribe points per developer, a fixed hours-to-points conversion, or a universal buffer.

The [Sprint Capacity Calculator](/tools/sprint-capacity-calculator) uses available days and an entered points-per-day planning assumption. Its recommended result applies an 80 percent factor to the entered point total. Treat that as a heuristic to discuss with the team.

For the calendar in this example, Alex has seven available days before meetings and support, Blair has eight, and the other three people have nine each. Use the hours table when you need to account explicitly for meeting and support time. The calculator does not infer those commitments from a person's name or role.

A default rate shown in a calculator is an example input. There is no general junior, mid-level, or senior developer points-per-day benchmark. [Story points describe relative work and uncertainty](https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/estimation); agree on any planning assumption using the team's own context and review it against completed work.

## Run a ten minute capacity check

1. Confirm the Sprint dates and scheduled hours for each person.
2. Mark holidays and leave once, including part-time schedules.
3. Record planned meetings, support duties, and other commitments.
4. Explain any reserve and check for double counting.
5. Compare the proposed work with recent delivery and role constraints.
6. Agree on the Sprint Goal and make remaining uncertainty visible.

Use the [Sprint Planning Checklist](/tools/sprint-planning-checklist) for the readiness discussion. Keep your capacity assumptions with the plan so a later retrospective can distinguish unexpected change from a planning mistake.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do you account for a public holiday?

Subtract the hours that each affected person would otherwise have worked. If your working-day count already excludes the holiday, make no further holiday deduction.

### Does a person on leave reduce the team forecast proportionally?

Not necessarily. Work dependencies, role coverage, and the selected stories affect the outcome. Make the absence visible, then discuss the specific plan rather than assuming every person contributes an interchangeable fraction of points.

### Should every team use a 20 percent buffer?

No. Choose an explicit reserve using the team's interruption history and uncertainty. Avoid counting the same interruption allowance in both the reserve and known commitments.

### What should a new team do without velocity history?

Use known availability, select a small coherent set of work, and make uncertainty visible. Inspect what actually finishes and build a useful history over time. An experience-based points quota does not replace that learning.
